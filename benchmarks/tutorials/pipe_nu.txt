I want the simplest case in CFD that has an exact answer, as a check that I can trust this
thing before I point it at anything real. Laminar flow in a circular pipe, air, Re = 100
on the diameter, with the wall held at a constant temperature above the fluid: the
classical Graetz problem, whose fully developed Nusselt number is 3.6568. That is the
first eigenvalue of the Graetz problem halved, lambda_1^2 / 2 with lambda_1^2 = 7.3136,
and it is exact rather than a correlation. Please compare against 3.6568 and not against
the textbook rounding of 3.66, which is 0.09% high and would manufacture a bias of its own
at the accuracy I am asking about. Note also that 4.364, which is 48/11, is the
constant-heat-flux answer and not this one; the two get swapped constantly.

Pipe diameter 0.02 m and length 0.3 m, so 15 diameters; mean velocity 0.075 m/s; Prandtl
number 0.70. An axisymmetric wedge is fine and is what I would use.

Give me the fully developed Nusselt number, averaged over the region where the flow and
the temperature field are both genuinely fully developed, and tell me where you took the
average and why that region qualifies: the hydrodynamic and the thermal entrance lengths
for these numbers, worked out rather than asserted. Nu = h D / k with
h = q_wall / (T_wall - T_bulk), and I care that T_bulk is the mixed mean and not the area
average, because in a duct the fluid at the wall is the hot fluid, the two differ, and
only one of them is what the Nusselt number is defined on.

One thing I would like you to be honest about rather than tidy. The answer depends on how
you take the wall temperature gradient and on where you put the cell centre of a graded
radial cell, and on a mesh like this those choices move the number by a few tenths of a
percent, which is the same size as the error you are reporting. So tell me which stencil
you used, and if it is cheap, tell me what the answer would have been with a different one.
I would rather know the number is good to half a percent for a stated reason than be given
a third significant figure that belongs to the post-processing.

Last: how you know the solve converged, what mesh you used, and what it cost.
