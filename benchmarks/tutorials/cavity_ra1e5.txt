I want the standard buoyancy benchmark as a check on the buoyant solver: natural
convection of air in a square cavity at Ra = 1e5 and Pr = 0.71, the case de Vahl Davis
published as a benchmark numerical solution in 1983. A square cavity of side 0.1 m, the
two vertical walls held at different fixed temperatures, the top and bottom adiabatic,
two-dimensional, steady.

Be careful which Nusselt number you are comparing with, because this paper reports
several and secondary sources collapse them into one. His Table V at Ra = 1e5 gives an
average Nusselt number of **4.509 on the hot wall** (his Nu_0), and **4.519** for both the
average through the whole cavity (his Nu) and the average on the vertical mid-plane (his
Nu_1/2), which coincide to four figures at this Rayleigh number. They are 0.22% apart,
which matters here because his own estimated accuracy at Ra = 1e5 is 0.3%. I want the
hot-wall average against 4.509, and I want you to say which definition you computed. If
you also give me the mid-plane value against 4.519, better: he recommends that one as the
number to describe heat flow across a cavity with adiabatic ends.

Set the Rayleigh number by choosing the temperature difference rather than by bending a
property: pick dT so that Ra = g beta dT L^3 / (nu alpha) comes out at 1e5 with air's own
beta, nu and Pr, and show me that arithmetic including the alpha you used. I would rather
have an odd-looking dT and honest properties than round numbers and a Rayleigh number
that is secretly 8e4.

Give me the hot-wall Nusselt number and also the **cold**-wall one. With the top and
bottom adiabatic and the flow steady, every watt entering through one vertical wall has to
leave through the other exactly, so the two agreeing is a closed energy balance and the
two disagreeing means the run has not converged whatever the residuals say. Tell me how
far apart they are. Say which reference temperature and which length went into
Nu = h L / k and how you took the wall gradient, because on a graded mesh the stencil
moves the answer by more than you would like.

If you want two more checks that are free, his Table V also gives the maximum horizontal
velocity on the vertical mid-plane as 34.73 at height 0.855 and the maximum vertical
velocity on the horizontal mid-plane as 68.59 at 0.066 from the hot wall, both
non-dimensionalised on alpha/L and not on nu/L. Getting those two scales mixed up is a
factor of Pr and it is the usual reason a correct solve appears to fail this benchmark.

Then tell me the local Nusselt number distribution along the hot wall and whether its
shape is the one this flow should have, how you know the solve reached a steady state
rather than merely stopping, what mesh you used, and what it cost.
