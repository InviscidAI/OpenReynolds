I want the reattachment length behind a backward-facing step, on the case Gartling set up
as a 2D test problem in 1990, and I want the units nailed down before anything is solved,
because this is the case where everybody gets them wrong.

Gartling's geometry, in his own non-dimensionalisation: the downstream channel runs
-0.5 <= y <= 0.5, so its height H is 1. The inlet is the upper half of the left boundary
only, 0 < y <= 0.5, carrying u = 24 y (0.5 - y), whose peak is 1.5 and whose mean over the
inlet opening is exactly 1. The lower half of the left boundary is the step face, so the
step height is H/2 = 0.5. The channel is 30H long downstream, the walls are no-slip and
the outflow is traction-free. Laminar and steady.

His Reynolds number is Re = u_mean H / nu on the **downstream** channel height, not on the
step height and not on the inlet height, so Re = 800 means nu = 1/800 = 0.00125 with
u_mean = 1 and H = 1. Note what that implies: because the expansion ratio is exactly 2,
H is also the hydraulic diameter of the inlet channel, so Re 800 here is the same flow
rate as Armaly's 1983 experiment at its Re_Dh = 800, and it is Re 400 on the inlet height.
Please write the Reynolds number you used, the length you put in it and the viscosity that
follows, and tell me if you think I have any of that wrong.

Then give me the reattachment length on the lower wall from the sign changes of the wall
shear along it, not by eye off a picture, and give it to me **twice**: in channel heights
H and in step heights. Gartling's own value is 6.1, and I want to know which of those two
numbers that 6.1 is comparable to, from your own reading of the normalisation rather than
from my say-so. A reattachment length compared across those two units is wrong by a
factor of two while looking plausible.

List every sign change you find, not just the one you pick. The first is the corner eddy
and is not the answer, and something further downstream may be a secondary bubble on the
upper wall or may be an artifact. Gartling also reports an upper-wall separation bubble,
so tell me whether you found one and where it starts and ends.

Last, and I care about this more than the number: tell me whether a steady solution
exists at this Reynolds number, and what your residuals and your reattachment length
actually did over the run rather than whether they met a tolerance. If the number is
still moving when you stop, say so and show me the drift, because a drifting number
quoted to three figures is worse than no number.
